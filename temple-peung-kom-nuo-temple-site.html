ពើងគំនូរ គឺជាទីតាំងសាសនាបុរាណមួយដែលស្ថិតនៅតំបន់ភ្នំគូលែន។ នៅសម័យបុរាណ ទីនេះត្រូវបានហៅថា «មហេន្ទ្រគុហា» ហើយសិលាចារឹកបានរៀបរាប់ថា គុហានេះស្ថិតនៅលើភ្នំមហេន្ទ្របព៌ត ដែលជាឈ្មោះបុរាណពាក់ព័ន្ធនឹងភ្នំគូលែន។

English Name: Peung Kom Nuo / Peung Komnou
Location: Trapeang Tuem Village, Ta Siem Commune, Svay Leu District, Siem Reap Province, Cambodia, in the Phnom Kulen area.
Date / Period: Angkorian period. The site contains an important inscription, K.176, dated to Saka 996, corresponding to 1074 CE, during the reign of King Harshavarman III (1066–1080). The exact dates of all the rock carvings are not certain, and some may be older than the inscription.

History
Peung Kom Nuo is a remarkable sacred rock-shelter and ancient religious site associated with the Phnom Kulen mountain region. In ancient times, the cave was known as Mahendra Kuha, meaning the cave of Mahendra, and the inscription describes it as being on Mount Mahendraparvata, the ancient name associated with Phnom Kulen.

The site is particularly important because of its connection with the Brahmin Sivasoma, a royal teacher associated with King Indravarman I of the late ninth century. The inscription tells of Sivasoma and his religious practice and records that sacred images were created in the cave for devotion to the supreme deity. A separate Old Khmer inscription on the rock gives the name “Vraḥ Śivasoma.”

Although the inscription was carved in 1074, it refers to an earlier religious tradition and an important Brahmanical figure from the ninth century. This suggests that Peung Kom Nuo remained a significant sacred place across different generations of the Angkorian period.

Description
Unlike a conventional temple with towers and galleries, Peung Kom Nuo is centered on natural sandstone rock shelters and large rock surfaces carved with Hindu religious imagery. The site combines natural landscape, sculpture, inscriptions, and evidence of ancient religious activity.

What Visitors Can See
Visitors can see impressive rock carvings including an eight-armed Ganesha, Vishnu reclining on Ananta, Vishnu riding Garuda, and groups of Hindu deities, divine attendants, guardians, and ascetic figures. Rock surfaces also contain inscriptions, while some areas preserve traces of painted rock art.

Peung Kom Nuo offers a quieter and more adventurous experience than Cambodia's major temple complexes, combining ancient Khmer religious art with the natural environment of Phnom Kulen.
