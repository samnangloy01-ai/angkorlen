English History

Prasat Trung Lolok is a small and little-known ancient Khmer temple located in the Beng Mealea area of Siem Reap Province, west of National Road 64 and close to the larger monument of Beng Mealea. It belongs to a group of lesser-known archaeological sites scattered through the forest and agricultural landscape surrounding Beng Mealea. ANGKOR TEMPLES IN CAMBODIA

Unlike Beng Mealea, which is a large and complex temple, Prasat Trung Lolok is a much smaller sanctuary. Today, the monument is extremely difficult to approach because of dense jungle vegetation and thorny growth. Documentation of the site reports that only the upper courses of a small square stone prasat can readily be seen above the vegetation. ANGKOR TEMPLES IN CAMBODIA

The surviving stonework indicates that the principal sanctuary was built as a square structure. Around the central ruin, scattered brick fragments have also been observed. These bricks may represent smaller structures that once formed part of the religious complex, or they may belong to an enclosure surrounding the sanctuary. Because so little of the original plan survives, its complete layout cannot presently be established with certainty. ANGKOR TEMPLES IN CAMBODIA

Prasat Trung Lolok is particularly interesting because of its position among several other small monuments west of Beng Mealea. Nearby archaeological remains include Prasat Kuk Troap, Prasat Daun Chan, Prasat Chrei, and other lesser-known sites. Together, these monuments demonstrate that the landscape around Beng Mealea once contained a much wider network of religious establishments than the famous central temple alone suggests. ANGKOR TEMPLES IN CAMBODIA

The exact date of construction and the ruler responsible for Prasat Trung Lolok are not securely established in the sources currently available. For this reason, it is safer to describe the temple as an ancient Khmer religious monument associated with the Beng Mealea archaeological landscape rather than assigning it to a particular king or century without stronger evidence.

Its present condition also illustrates an important problem facing many lesser-known Khmer monuments. While the major temples receive conservation and visitor infrastructure, small forest sanctuaries such as Trung Lolok can become almost completely hidden by vegetation. Nevertheless, even these fragmentary remains are valuable for understanding how Khmer religious sites were distributed throughout the countryside.

For AngkorLens, Prasat Trung Lolok is therefore best presented as a little-known Khmer sanctuary near Beng Mealea, emphasizing its surviving architecture, forest setting, and relationship to the wider archaeological landscape rather than claiming an uncertain construction date.
