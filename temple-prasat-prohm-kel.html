Prasat Prohm Kel

House of fire · Chong Kal, Oddar Meanchey

Prasat Prohm Kel — ប្រាសាទព្រហ្មកិល

Location: Cheung Tien commune, Chong Kal district, Oddar Meanchey province — about 700 m north of Spean Toap, the great ancient bridge on the Sreng River, and about 35 km from Samraong town.

Prohm Kel is a small Bayon-style sanctuary from the late 12th or early 13th century, the reign of King Jayavarman VII. It is built of laterite with sandstone door frames and windows. The single tower faces east; its south side has windows while the north side has none — the typical plan of the "houses of fire" (dharmasala / vahni-griha), the rest-house chapels Jayavarman VII placed along the ancient Royal Road from Angkor to Phimai (in present-day Thailand). Two old basins lie beside the temple, and the bridges Spean Toap, Spean Tbong and Spean Memay are nearby on the same road.

The temple was recorded by the French more than a century ago: by Lunet de Lajonquière in 1911 (Inventaire Khmer no. 721) and in the 1926 list of historic monuments, which noted a laterite building, basins and statues. In my photographs the tower still rises in stepped laterite tiers, reflected in the pond in front, with carved sandstone pieces lined up at its base. The temple has never been restored — what you see is the original structure as it has survived.
