Preah Stoeng Terrace

Preah Stoeng Terrace

English: Preah Stoeng Terrace

🇬🇧 English History

Prasat Preah Stoeng Terrace is a remarkable architectural feature of Preah Khan Kampong Svay, located on the western side of the great baray (reservoir). Prasat Preah Stoeng itself is famous for its central tower decorated with four large faces in the artistic style associated with the reign of King Jayavarman VII. The sanctuary is positioned at the northwestern side of the baray and forms an important part of the larger religious landscape of Preah Khan Kampong Svay. UNESCO World Heritage Centre

The terrace lies on the eastern side of Prasat Preah Stoeng, extending toward the baray. Historical descriptions record a large rectangular platform leading eastward to a cruciform terrace at the water's edge. This means the terrace was not simply a decorative platform in front of the temple; it formed an architectural connection between the sanctuary and the great reservoir. Hello Angkor

One of the most extraordinary features of the terrace is its stone decoration. The terrace walls are carved with repeated images of Hamsa, the sacred swan or celestial goose commonly associated with purity, wisdom, and the divine world in South and Southeast Asian traditions. At the corners are especially unusual three-headed Hamsa (tri-headed Hamsa) motifs. These carvings make the terrace one of the most distinctive surviving architectural elements at Preah Khan Kampong Svay. Hello Angkor

The relationship between the terrace, the temple, and the baray was likely important both architecturally and symbolically. The large reservoir was one of the major hydraulic features of Preah Khan Kampong Svay, measuring roughly 600 × 3,000 metres according to the UNESCO Tentative List. The terrace therefore created a formal approach from the sacred temple toward the water landscape. UNESCO World Heritage Centre

Today, the terrace remains an important example of Khmer architectural creativity. Its combination of a monumental approach, connection to the baray, and unusual Hamsa carvings provides valuable evidence of the sophisticated religious and artistic environment of Preah Khan Kampong Svay.
