English: Ancient Drainage System with Makara Sculpture

🇬🇧 English History

The Ancient Drainage System with the Makara Sculpture is a remarkable example of ancient Khmer hydraulic engineering located in Teuk Lech Village, Boeung Mealea Commune, Svay Leu District, Siem Reap Province, approximately 3 km southwest of Beng Mealea Temple. Unlike a conventional temple, this site was part of a larger water-management system designed to collect, control, and direct water through the landscape.

The most distinctive feature is the large Makara, a mythical aquatic creature commonly represented in Khmer art and architecture. At this site, the Makara was not simply decorative. Its mouth formed part of the water outlet, allowing water traveling through the ancient channel to flow outward through the creature's mouth. This combination of religious imagery and practical engineering is an important characteristic of Khmer sacred landscapes.

According to the APSARA National Authority, the ancient channel was designed to collect water coming from the Phnom Kulen area. The water was directed through the drainage structure and emerged from the Makara's mouth before continuing toward O Thmor Dap. From there, the water continued toward the water system associated with Beng Mealea, and ultimately toward the large reservoir east of the temple known as Veal Phtei.

This system demonstrates that ancient Khmer builders understood the importance of controlling water across considerable distances. Water was essential not only for agriculture and settlement but also for maintaining the hydraulic landscapes surrounding major religious centers such as Beng Mealea.

Over the centuries, water erosion, natural deterioration, vegetation, and the movement of supporting stones caused the Makara structure to deteriorate severely. Before restoration, parts of the Makara's body had shifted from their original positions and the channel no longer functioned properly.

In 2024, the APSARA National Authority completed the first phase of restoration, covering approximately 18 meters of the drainage structure. Archaeologists and conservation specialists documented scattered stones, excavated the area, reinforced damaged stones, and reconstructed the Makara according to its original arrangement. Special attention was given to protecting the foundation from further water erosion.

Today, the Makara drainage system provides an important window into the relationship between Khmer architecture, mythology, water management, and the natural landscape surrounding Beng Mealea.

ចំណុចពិសេសបំផុតរបស់ទីតាំងនេះ គឺ ចម្លាក់សត្វមករ ដែលជាសត្វទេវកថាក្នុងសិល្បៈខ្មែរ។ នៅទីនេះ មករមិនមែនមានតួនាទីសម្រាប់តែការតុបតែងនោះទេ ប៉ុន្តែបានក្លាយជាផ្នែកមួយនៃប្រព័ន្ធបង្ហូរទឹក ដោយទឹកអាចហូរចេញតាម មាត់មករ។

តាមការសិក្សារបស់អាជ្ញាធរជាតិអប្សរា ប្រព័ន្ធនេះត្រូវបានប្រើសម្រាប់ប្រមូលទឹកពីតំបន់ ភ្នំគូលែន ហើយបញ្ជូនទឹកចុះមកតាមចង្អូរបង្ហូរ។ ទឹកបានហូរចេញតាមមាត់មករ ហើយបន្តទៅ អូរថ្មដាប់។ បន្ទាប់មក ទឹកបន្តទៅបំពេញប្រព័ន្ធទឹកនៅតំបន់ ប្រាសាទបេងមាលា និងបន្តទៅអាងរក្សាទឹកធំមួយនៅខាងកើតប្រាសាទ ដែលគេហៅថា វាលផ្ទី។

ប្រព័ន្ធនេះបង្ហាញពីចំណេះដឹងដ៏សំខាន់របស់បុព្វបុរសខ្មែរ ក្នុងការគ្រប់គ្រងទឹក និងរៀបចំទេសភាព។ ទឹកមានសារៈសំខាន់សម្រាប់ការរស់នៅ កសិកម្ម និងសម្រាប់ប្រព័ន្ធសាសនាដែលនៅជុំវិញប្រាសាទធំៗ ដូចជា បេងមាលា។

ដោយសារការហូរច្រោះរបស់ទឹក អាយុកាល ដើមឈើ និងការរំកិលថ្ម គ្រឹះ និងផ្នែកខ្លះនៃចម្លាក់មករបានខូចខាតយ៉ាងខ្លាំង។ មុនពេលជួសជុល ផ្នែកខ្លួនមករខ្លះបានរើចេញពីទីតាំងដើម ហើយប្រព័ន្ធបង្ហូរទឹកលែងដំណើរការដូចសម័យបុរាណ។

នៅឆ្នាំ ២០២៤ អាជ្ញាធរជាតិអប្សរាបានបញ្ចប់ដំណាក់កាលទី១ នៃការជួសជុល ដែលមានប្រវែងប្រហែល ១៨ ម៉ែត្រ។ ក្រុមអ្នកជំនាញបានសិក្សា និងរៀបចំថ្មដែលរាយប៉ាយ ជួសជុលថ្មដែលខូច និងរៀបចំផ្នែកខ្លួនមករឡើងវិញតាមលំនាំដើម។

សព្វថ្ងៃ ទីតាំងនេះគឺជាភស្តុតាងដ៏មានតម្លៃដែលបង្ហាញពីការរួមបញ្ចូលគ្នារវាង ស្ថាបត្យកម្មខ្មែរ ជំនឿលើសត្វទេវកថា បច្ចេកទេសគ្រប់គ្រងទឹក និងទេសភាពធម្មជាតិ ក្នុងអរិយធម៌ខ្មែរ។
