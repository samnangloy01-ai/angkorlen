Daun Mea Temple (Preah Pithu V)

ប្រាសាទដូនមារ

Preah Pithu V · Angkor Thom, Siem Reap

Photographs by Samnang Loy · AngkorLens

Daun Mea Temple — ប្រាសាទដូនមារ (Preah Pithu V; also written Duan Mea or "Temple v")

Location: Angkor Thom, Sangkat Nokor Thum, Siem Reap. Daun Mea stands near the middle of the Preah Pithu group, five temples (T, U, V, X and Y) that were not necessarily built at the same time. Wikipedia places it north of Temple U, outside the remains of the moat.

Hello Angkor dates the temple to the early 12th century, judged from its art style because there is no foundation inscription, in the era of Angkor Wat. It is a Hindu temple, but the god it was dedicated to is not stated. APSARA's page on the Preah Pithu group gives the beginning of the 12th century and lists the reign of Suryavarman II for the group. The carving is fine but unfinished in places, and there are no large devata figures or medallions here, unlike some other temples of the group.

What to see: the temple is built on an earthen mound. A large terrace and raised causeway form a grand entrance on the west, with round columns of the kind also seen at Baphuon, Beng Mealea, Chau Say Tevoda, Banteay Samre and the Royal Palace terrace. Researchers disagree on whether the terrace was added later or built with the temple, and one study suggests a late or post-Bayon date for it. The plan is cruciform with a hall (mandapa) extending east, and steps lead up to open entrances on the north and south sides. Monumental stone pillars on the east side have been moved from their original places. Wikipedia describes the sanctuary standing on a two-tier basement, the largest sanctuary chamber of the group, 3.80 m square, holding a large linga of about 1.5 m. Hello Angkor places a large sandstone linga on a pedestal base in the northern wing, possibly once at the centre of the shrine. In my photographs, a linga is seen through a doorway with a timber frame, and balustered windows are visible on the upper walls.

Inscriptions and records: there is no foundation inscription; inscription K. 841 is a single line whose script and date are unknown. Aymonier mentioned the group in 1904 and Lajonquière listed Temple v in 1911; it is in the 1926 list of Historic Monuments (no. 374, three terraces and three sandstone sanctuaries) with IK 484 and CISARK 384. Wikipedia says the Preah Pithu temples were first cleared by Jean Commaille in 1908 and then by Henri Marchal from 1918 to 1920.
