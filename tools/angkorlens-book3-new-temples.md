## Notes per temple

### 1. Prasat Ta Dong (ប្រាសាទតាដុង)
- Brick tower in dry forest and bamboo; grass and small trees growing on top.
- False door of brick panels; lintel carved directly into the brick (leaf scrolls, small figures).
- One side supported by metal scaffolding.
- Octagonal sandstone colonnettes and broken pieces lying around.
- Nearby rock shelter with offerings and portrait of a revered monk.
- Also called Prasat Tadong / Ta Ong. Location: side of Phnom Kulen, Chop Leu village, Svay Leu commune/district, Siem Reap (helloangkor.com).
- Originally 3 brick towers; 1 remains, faces east; false upper levels; outer brick wall with east gopura; well; sandstone remains; ruined laterite stair; hermit caves nearby.
- Recorded by Aymonier 1900, Lajonquière 1902 (IK 226), Parmentier 1913 ("Prasat Ta Don").
- Dating: lintels "reminiscent of 7th-century style" (helloangkor); 9th c. / Jayavarman II / Kulen style per a Facebook post — verify before the book.
- Still to confirm: deity, monk's name in shrine.

### 2. Spean Ta Ong (ស្ពានតាអុង) — also Spean Ta Oung / Ta Oak
- Location: Chong Spean village, Khvav commune, Chi Kraeng district, Siem Reap (APSARA, helloangkor).
- On the ancient East Royal Road: Angkor – Beng Mealea – Preah Khan Kampong Svay; crosses the Ta Ong stream; still in daily use.
- APSARA: 3rd-longest Angkorian bridge after Spean Toap and Kampong Kdei (same article also says "longest of 23" on the road — conflicting).
- Size: 63 m × 12 m × 7.3 m (helloangkor) vs 77 m between abutments, 13 m wide (Lajonquière 1902). 14 corbelled openings.
- Laterite with sandstone naga balustrades; naga-head fans among the finest.
- Date: between Suryavarman I and Suryavarman II, likely II; possible Jayavarman VII additions (helloangkor).
- APSARA repairs 2021 and 2022.

### 3. Tonle Snguot Temple (ប្រាសាទទន្លេស្ងួត)
- North of Angkor Thom; Nokor Krau village, Sangkat Kouk Chak, Siem Reap city.
- Hospital chapel (arogyasala) of Jayavarman VII, one of 102; one of four around Angkor Thom. Late 12th–early 13th c., Bayon style (APSARA).
- Sandstone tower with entrance hall, laterite enclosure, east gopura, library traces, NE basin; faces east to a reservoir (~800 × 400 m).
- Finds: stele K. 955 (Sanskrit, Saka 1108), K. 1112 graffiti, new 55-line Sanskrit stele (2020), dvarapala, ceramics, Medicine Buddha (now Preah Norodom Sihanouk-Angkor Museum).
- APSARA restoration 2019–2020; dam/reservoir works 2025 (NZ-supported Angkor Water Resilience).
- Photos show new grey replacement stones beside old ones; devatas; offerings inside.

### 4. Thma Dap Temple (ប្រាសាទថ្មដាប់)
- Anlong Thom village, Khnang Phnum commune, Svay Leu district, Siem Reap; Phnom Kulen group (helloangkor).
- Single brick sanctuary; carved brick pilasters, friezes, lintel, false floors; original stucco partly preserved.
- East: laterite steps, remains of outer enclosure. Surveyed 1973, 1979.
- Photos: crack down the front, wooden support frame, vegetation on top; nearby stream + waterfall.
- Age: 1,200+ years (APSARA, 2 Jan 2026); early Angkorian Kulen period, 9th c., Jayavarman II era (user info; no inscription).
- APSARA restoration from Jan 2026, 4 months: cracks E & W, sandstone doorway/lintel/pillars, vegetation removed. First structural restoration ever.
- Photos taken before restoration.
- Still to confirm: deity.

### 5. Bakong Temple (ប្រាសាទបាគង) — not in Books 1 & 2
- Roluos group, ancient capital Hariharalaya, Siem Reap.
- State temple of King Indravarman I, dedicated to Shiva in 881 CE; one of the first great Khmer temple-mountains.
- Sandstone pyramid of 5 levels; stone lions on stairways, elephants at corners of lower terraces.
- Two moats; causeway with 7-headed naga balustrades (among the earliest).
- Summit tower added later (12th c.). Restored by Maurice Glaize in the 1930s by anastylosis.
- Photos: devatas on summit tower, carved lintel framing the tower, moat with palms.
- Source: Wikipedia — Bakong.

### 6. Banteay Srei (ប្រាសាទបន្ទាយស្រី) — not in Books 1 & 2
- North of the main Angkor group; "citadel of women / of beauty".
- Foundation inscription: 967 CE, reign of Rajendravarman II; built by Brahmin advisor Yajnavaraha and brother Vishnukumara; dedicated to Shiva.
- Pink sandstone; three central towers; finest carving in Khmer art.
- Pediments: Vali vs Sugriva with Rama (Ramayana); Indra on 3-headed elephant sending rain on the burning Khandava forest (Mahabharata); Narasimha killing Hiranyakashipu.
- Rediscovered 1914; André Malraux tried to remove devatas 1923; Henri Marchal anastylosis from 1931.
- Sources: Sahapedia; Wikipedia — Banteay Srei.

### 7. Spean Toap (ស្ពានទ័ព) — also Spean Tip / O'Chik Bridge
- Cheung Tien village & commune, Chong Kal district, Oddar Meanchey; over the Sreng River.
- Longest ancient bridge of the Khmer Empire (helloangkor); ~150 m long, 14.5–16.4 m wide, ~10 m high, 28 openings (ron ta dev).
- Jayavarman VII, late 12th–early 13th c. (The Star / Ministry of Culture).
- On the NW Royal Road Angkor–Phimai; near Spean Memay and Spean Tbong.
- Laterite; sandstone naga balustrades and end caps.
- Ministry of Culture conservation since 2015 (8 phases). Bombed at both ends 15 Dec 2025 during border fighting; 68 balustrade sections damaged; inspected 12 Sep 2026.
- Photos taken during conservation (crane, bamboo scaffolding), before the damage.

### 8. Prasat Prohm Kel (ប្រាសាទព្រហ្មកិល)
- Cheung Tien commune, Chong Kal district, Oddar Meanchey; ~700 m north of Spean Toap; ~35 km from Samraong (helloangkor).
- Late 12th–early 13th c., Jayavarman VII, Bayon style.
- Laterite with sandstone door frames/windows; single tower facing east; windows on south side only, none on north.
- "House of fire" (dharmasala / vahni-griha) on the NW Royal Road Angkor–Phimai. Two basins nearby.
- Recorded: Lajonquière 1911 (IK 721); MH 580 (1926): "laterite building, basins and statues".
- Photos: stepped laterite tower reflected in pond; sandstone pieces lined at base; info sign board.
- Never restored (confirmed by Samnang, visited in person).
- Still to confirm: village name (helloangkor gives Cheung Tien and Srae Prang).

### 9. Spean Preah Chang'er (ស្ពានព្រះចង្អេរ) — also Spean Prah Changer
- Kôl village & commune, Angkor Chum district, Siem Reap (helloangkor).
- Laterite; 47 m long, 9 m wide; at least 12 corbelled openings; 12th–13th c. (no king named in source).
- NW Royal Road Angkor–Phimai; Kdei Ta Kom group.
- Lajonquière 1911 (IK 687; then "Srok Kralanh"); MH 555 (1926); CISARK 561.
- Still used by local traffic; newer road parallel on east.
- Photos: laterite courses with lifting holes, reflections, arch openings at waterline; 2 photos with visitors on top.
- Still to confirm: king; restoration history.

### 10. Prasat Preah Phnom (ប្រាសាទព្រះភ្នំ) — "The Holy Mountain"
- Preah Phnom village, Srae Khvav commune, Angkor Chum district, Siem Reap; ~65 km from Siem Reap (helloangkor).
- 10th c. (no king named in source). 5 square brick towers on a natural mound, facing east, 2 rows (3 front, 2 back); 2 brick annexes; moat; basin to north; no wall/gopura.
- Sandstone door frames, octagonal colonnettes; lintels worn — central one: Indra on 3-headed elephant. Corbelled roofs standing; grass on top.
- Inscriptions: K.593 (Saka 852 = 930 CE), K.454 (Saka 1023 = 1101 CE, central tower), K.594 (15 lines).
- Lajonquière 1901 (IK 706); MH 565 (1926); CISARK 571.
- Large Nandin and pedestal fragments reported on site.
- Never restored (confirmed by Samnang, visited in person); door frames are original.
- Still to confirm: deity (Shiva? Nandin suggests).

### 11. Prasat Kok Chak (ប្រាសាទគោកចក) — also Kouk Chak / Prasat Chak
- Teaksen Tbong village, Sangkat Kouk Chak, Siem Reap town; ~200 m north of Wat Prasat Kouk Chak (helloangkor).
- 2 brick sanctuaries, N–S line, facing east; sandstone door frames; octagonal colonnettes; former moat (dry).
- Lintels (from photos): one = Garuda on naga heads holding garland; other = crowned figure above two small lions, makaras at ends — identify the figure before the book.
- Inscription K.521 on door jambs (Khmer, 15 + 16 lines; IC IV p.167).
- Date conflict: helloangkor "10th century" but also "foundation 850 AD" from K.521 — check Coedès before printing.
- IK 544.01; CISARK 431; MH 420 (1926, "Prasat Chak").
- APSARA restored/strengthened brickwork (new orange bricks visible).
- Living worship: coconuts, lotus, incense, offerings at the main tower door.

### 11. Prasat Kok Chak (ប្រាសាទគោកចក) — also Kouk Chak / Prasat Chak
- Teaksen Tbong village, Sangkat Kouk Chak, Siem Reap city; ~200 m north of modern Wat Prasat Kouk Chak (helloangkor).
- 2 brick sanctuaries, N–S line, both facing east; dry moat.
- Sandstone door frames, octagonal colonnettes (very fine bands); lintels: south/main tower = Garuda holding nagas; other tower = crowned figure on garland with monster heads (identity not confirmed).
- Inscription K.521 on door jambs (Khmer, 15 + 16 lines; IC IV p.167).
- Date: 850 CE, 9th century (foundation date from inscription K.521; chosen by Samnang). helloangkor also lists "10th century".
- IK 544.01; CISARK 431; MH 420 (1926, "Prasat Chak").
- APSARA: work began 2015 (south tower + south wall); Feb 2021 announced both towers repaired (Khmer Times 11 Feb 2021). New bright bricks visible in photos.
- Photos: offerings (coconuts, lotus, baysei) at main door; false doors on other sides.
- Still to confirm: king, deity.

### 12. Kor Sak Temple (ប្រាសាទកោរសក់) — Preah Pithu T / "Temple t"
- Angkor Thom, Sangkat Nokor Thum, Siem Reap; northeast of the ancient Royal Palace. One of 5 temples of the Preah Pithu group (T, U, V, X, Y); shares a moated island with Chorm Temple (Preah Pithu U) to the east (helloangkor).
- Khmer name used on site: ប្រាសាទកោរសក់ (Samnang). helloangkor writes ព្រះពិធូរ (ប៉ម តេ); one travel site spells it ប្រាសាទកោសក់ — spelling to confirm before the book.
- Date: early 12th c. (helloangkor); APSARA group page: beginning of 12th c., Angkor Wat style, reign Suryavarman II (for the group). helloangkor names no king. APSARA: the 5 temples were not built together. Wikipedia: T and U probably built together (same axis, one moat).
- Originally Vishnu (helloangkor). Reused sandstone blocks from 11th- and 13th-c. sites inside the enclosure.
- Faces west; large raised cruciform terrace with naga balustrade; west gopura -> central stepped pyramid shrine with a staircase on each side; east gopura -> Chorm; small N and S entrances; 2 devatas on each side of the outer walls. Fully moated; reached only by the west causeway.
- Finds: linga with inscription K.544 (Sanskrit, deity names; Phnom Penh National Museum, ka 1687); K.545 (one Khmer line on a pedestal); 3 lingas reported.
- Records: Aymonier 1904 (Le Cambodge III, p.103); Lajonquiere 1911 ("Temple t", Inventaire III, p.66); BEFEO Chronique 1918; MH 374 (1926, "3 terraces and 3 sandstone sanctuaries"); IK 481; CISARK 381. Wikipedia: group cleared by Commaille 1908, Marchal 1918-20.
- Restoration: helloangkor (Chorm page) says work in the Preah Pithu group began in 2019 with Kor Sak's west terrace, then Chorm. Another site says a Korea-funded (KOICA / Korea Cultural Heritage Foundation + APSARA) project began in Sept 2015 — start date conflict, so only 2019 (helloangkor) is on the web page. APSARA says Korea Cultural Heritage Foundation is restoring Chorm (3 years from 2021). Scaffolding visible behind the naga terrace in Samnang's photos.
- Photos: main pyramid (cover), pyramid stairs with devata niche, naga terrace, 3 naga close-ups (lichen), 2 fig-root doorways.
- Still to confirm: king, which devata/lintel details, restoration dates.

### 13. Daun Mea Temple (ប្រាសាទដូនមារ) — Preah Pithu V / Duan Mea / "Temple v"
- Angkor Thom, Sangkat Nokor Thum, Siem Reap. Near the middle of the Preah Pithu group (T, U, V, X, Y); Wikipedia: north of Temple U, outside the residual moat. Spelling: Samnang writes Daun Mea; helloangkor writes Duan Mea; helloangkor Khmer name ព្រះពិធូរ (ប៉ម វេ) — confirm Khmer spelling ដូនមារ before the book.
- Date: early 12th c. from art style, no foundation inscription (helloangkor); APSARA group page: beginning of 12th c., Angkor Wat style, reign Suryavarman II (for the group). Hindu; dedication not stated (do not say Shiva/Vishnu). Fine carving, unfinished in places; no large devatas or medallions.
- Earthen mound; large west terrace/raised causeway with round columns (compared to Baphuon, Beng Mealea, Chau Say Tevoda, Banteay Samre, Royal Palace terrace). Terrace date disputed: later addition or contemporary; one study (Uchida, Sato, Cunin, Toyouchi 2013, Archaeometry 55, magnetic susceptibility) suggests late/post-Bayon.
- Cruciform plan, mandapa extending east; steps to open N and S entrances; monumental stone pillars on the east displaced.
- Wikipedia: sanctuary on 2-tier basement, largest chamber of the group, 3.80 m square, large linga ~1.5 m; faces east with double vestibule; west causeway leads to a 40 m cruciform terrace, no enclosure. Other sources (penang-traveltips, hikingproject) say the causeway is about 70 m / 40 m — length conflict, so no length is on the web page.
- helloangkor: large sandstone linga with pedestal base in the NORTHERN wing, possibly once at the shrine centre — location differs from Wikipedia; web page gives both, attributed.
- Records: Aymonier 1904 (Le Cambodge III, p.103); Lajonquiere 1911 (Inventaire III, p.70); MH 374 (1926, 3 terraces and 3 sandstone sanctuaries — whole group); IK 484; CISARK 384; K.841 (one line, script/date unknown; Revue des Arts Cambodgiens Oct 1935). Wikipedia: group cleared by Commaille 1908, Marchal 1918-20.
- Restoration: no source found for Temple V (Kor Sak/Chorm works are separate).
- Photos: sanctuary from below with balustered windows (cover), far view, steps, corner of ruined wall, door jamb with colonnette, naga fragment, doorway with timber frame and linga, linga close-up.
- Still to confirm: king, dedication, windows/door details, restoration.

### 14. Kandork Yeak Temple (ប្រាសាទកណ្តកយក្ស) — Preah Pithu Y / "Temple y"
- Angkor Thom, Sangkat Nokor Thum, Siem Reap. 5th temple of the Preah Pithu group (T, U, V, X, Y); Wikipedia: north of Temple V ("western closure temple"). Names: Samnang writes Kandork Yeak; helloangkor "Kandork Yeak Temple" / Preah Pithu Y. helloangkor Khmer name is given as ព្រះពិធូរ (ប៉ម អ៊ីការិច) — odd spelling, not used on the page; confirm the Khmer spelling ប្រាសាទកណ្តកយក្ស before the book.
- Date: helloangkor 12th c.; originally dedicated to Vishnu. Wikipedia: no dedication stated, dated between Angkor Wat and Bayon styles (Glaize); ancient-history-sites says Jacques thought it later than T, U, V. APSARA group page: beginning of 12th c., Angkor Wat style, reign Suryavarman II (whole group only). The page says "between Angkor Wat and Bayon" attributed to Wikipedia and does not name a king.
- Plan (helloangkor): elongated sandstone building on a large rise; front room joins a longer room through a short hall that also gives the N and S entrances; the other four temples are small cruciform. Wikipedia: no basement or stairways, earthen platform, larger chamber mostly collapsed; faces east; long mandapa, vestibule, sanctuary 3.5 m x 3.0 m with linga 0.95 m tall.
- CONFLICT (kept off the web page): where the two half pediments are. helloangkor: flank the N and S doorways. Wikipedia: on the western side. Also helloangkor has a false door on the west while Wikipedia says the temple faces east. The page gives no pediment location. Wikipedia names the scenes: north one = Krishna defeating the asura Bana; south one = three steps of Vamana. The page gives these scenes attributed to Wikipedia and does not say which photo is which.
- Also helloangkor: small pediments over doors; pilasters with motif roundels; small basin to the north with laterite edging; terrace structure to the east; unidentified mound in the NE corner with deposits including a linga and pedestals ("unknown" there).
- Records: Aymonier 1904 (Le Cambodge III, p.103); Lajonquiere 1911 (Inventaire III, p.71, "Temple y"); MH 374 (1926; entry says 3 terraces and 3 sandstone sanctuaries, which does not fit one building, so it is a group entry; the page only says the group is in the 1926 list); IK 485; CISARK 385. Wikipedia: group cleared by Commaille 1908, Marchal 1918-20.
- Restoration: no source found for Temple Y (Chorm/Kor Sak works are separate). Inscriptions: none found.
- Photos (22): whole temple (cover), carved pediments with Vishnu-type scenes and close-ups of figures, chariot wheel scene (3 close-ups), small door pediments, door with colonnettes, dancing figure on pilaster, roundels band, row of figures and animals. Zoomed photos were sent by Samnang already; none re-cropped.
- Still to confirm: king, which scene is on which side, name spelling, restoration.

### 15. North Khleang (ប្រាសាទឃ្លាំងខាងជើង) — Prasat Khleang Khang Cheung
- Angkor Thom, east side of the Royal Square, directly behind the 12 towers of Prasat Suor Prat, across the royal road from Royal Palace to Victory Gate. Twin of South Khleang on the opposite side of the road (helloangkor, Wikipedia).
- Khmer name used by Samnang: ប្រាសាទឃ្លាំងខាងជើង — matches helloangkor's "Other Names" list exactly (also Preah Kleang, Prasat Khlan, Práḥ Khleang). No spelling conflict.
- Date: helloangkor tags "11th Century" (no precise date). Builder/date CONFLICT (kept off the web page): Wikipedia gives two different accounts — (a) North Khleang attributed to King Jayaviravarman, South Khleang to his successor Suryavarman I; (b) a separate account says North Khleang was first built in wood under Rajendravarman II and later rebuilt in stone by Jayavarman V, probably before South Khleang. Wikipedia does not reconcile these. A third source (ancient-history-sites.com, secondary) says North Khleang was built "probably in the late tenth or early eleventh century," possibly replacing an earlier wooden structure linked to Rajendravarman II, and that two inscriptions in the North Khleang date to Suryavarman I's reign. The web page uses the broad, least-conflicting framing: "11th century, associated with Jayaviravarman and Suryavarman I."
- Central tower: helloangkor says its date is uncertain and it is "suspected to be a later addition" — not stated on the web page.
- Architecture (helloangkor): long north-south gallery, central chamber originally topped by a tower, entrance east/exit west into a courtyard; small cruciform shrine at courtyard center with entrances on all 4 sides; west entrance extended by a raised terrace with naga balustrade (remnants only); courtyard wall mostly gone; galleries originally had tiled wood roofs, small end chambers, balustraded windows; ornate base molding and door columns in places. Gave its name to the "Khleang style" (simple lintels, central kala; cf. Phimeanakas, Ta Keo).
- Purpose: name means "storeroom" but considered unlikely; an oath-of-allegiance inscription on one doorway suggests reception hall / lodging for nobles and foreign envoys (helloangkor, Wikipedia agree).
- Inscriptions: K.468 (13 lines Sanskrit + 22 lines Khmer, partly legible, IC III p.225) and K.542 (south doorframe per body text, but the helloangkor inscription table location is inconsistent — table lists sections N1/N2/N3/S1/S2). K.542's Sanskrit section (N1) dated Saka 928; body text separately cites 1006 AD for an inscription — not reconciled, kept off the page.
- Objects found (mid-1900s French conservators, per helloangkor): Ganesha statue, partial stone tortoise (consecration deposit), four-armed figure possibly Avalokiteshvara on a spouted pedestal, dvarapala, male deity, Vishnu + female deity group.
- Records: 1926 Liste des Monuments Historiques #372 ("Práḥ Khleang" — two rectangular galleries in laterite and sandstone, inscriptions on jambs and a stele, sandstone sanctuary behind). IK 480.02; CISARK 379; helloangkor Ref HA11663.
- A separate, smaller site called "Prasat, East of North Khleang" (3 small shrines) sits just east of North Khleang's lesser eastern exit — not covered here, different site.
- Restoration: no specific restoration-date source found; not stated on the page.
- Photos (20): cover = deep doorway view through two door frames with carved lintel above, well-lit (user confirmed this choice). 2 near-duplicate lintel-carving shots differentiated by zoom-cropping one. 4 photos with an overly warm/orange cast toned down (saturation + red-channel reduction). Also includes: corridor view, tower ruins from 2 angles, carved deity statue, column capital, pedestal/altar, relief panels, pediment carving, balustrade window with fern growth, 2 circular medallion carvings (one zoom-cropped to differentiate from the other).
- Still to confirm: exact builder/date attribution (3-way source conflict above), central tower date, precise inscription dating, restoration history.

### 16. Prasat Suor Prat N1 (ប្រាសាទ សួព្រ័ត្រខាងជើង) — North Tower 1 of 12
- Angkor Thom, east side of the Royal Square, ~180 m east of the Royal Palace terraces, in front of the North and South Khleangs. Twelve laterite/sandstone towers run north-south in two groups of 6, split by Victory Avenue; this submission is specifically the North 1 (N1) tower (only 2 photos sent).
- Khmer name used by Samnang: ប្រាសាទ សួព្រ័ត្រខាងជើង — matches helloangkor's "other names" field (ប្រាសាទ សួរព្រ័ត្រខាងជើង / "North Sour Prat"); minor romanization-only variants exist across sources (Suor/Sour/Suor Proat), not a real spelling conflict.
- Date CONFLICT (kept off the web page): earlier scholarship (and most popular sources, e.g. Wikipedia, renown-travel, theangkorguide) dates the towers to the late 12th/early 13th c., reign of Jayavarman VII, Bayon style. A 2005 archaeometric study (Uchida et al., cited by helloangkor) used stone sourcing, block size/technique, and radiocarbon dating of charcoal to propose an earlier date, late 11th to mid-12th c. (Angkor Wat period) — helloangkor states this is not settled. No inscription gives a foundation date or names a builder; earlier Jayavarman VII / Indravarman II attributions are not confirmed. The web page uses the safe range "11th-13th Century."
- Purpose: the name "Suor Prat" ("towers of the rope dancers") is a modern/folk name reflecting a local legend of tightrope performances between the towers at royal festivals — helloangkor explicitly notes there is no structural or archaeological evidence for this. Zhou Daguan (13th-c. Chinese envoy) recorded the towers may have been used to adjudicate disputes (the accused confined inside; the one who fell ill was deemed guilty) — a theory repeated across many tourist sites (penang-traveltips, canbypublications, tourismcambodia). Another theory (renown-travel, lonelyplanet) is that they served as reception stands for viewing festivals/parades on the plaza. No single theory is confirmed; page presents both briefly without picking a winner.
- Architecture (helloangkor): blocky towers with false diminishing levels for height, balustered side windows, a mandapa extending each entrance; built mainly of laterite with sandstone door/window frames and frontons. helloangkor notes the towers were never finished — frontons underdeveloped, no final rendering, built in haste. Entrances were walled up at the base, likely when the fronting terraces were added later.
- N1 restoration (the specific tower in these photos): found severely deteriorated with a large structural inclination; selected by the Japan-APSARA Safeguarding Angkor team (JASA/JSA, led by Waseda University) for a conservation project that fully dismantled the upper structure AND the foundation to study the masonry and the cause of the tilt before rebuilding with new techniques (geo-textile in the compacted foundation soil). Restoration/research work traced to ~2002 (UNESCO/Japan program), published as an academic paper in 2009 (Akazawa et al., AIJ Journal of Technology and Design). Separately, JSA's broader Royal Plaza work ran 1994-2005 and restored 2 of the 12 towers by 2005.
- Excavation history: cleared by Commaille in 1908; one tower restored by EFEO in the 1950s; JSA studied/restored 2 towers 1994-2005 (N1 among the identified restoration subjects per the academic paper title). No inscriptions found anywhere at the 12-tower complex (helloangkor, confirmed).
- Records: 1926 Liste des Monuments Historiques #373 ("Prasat Suor Prat — Twelve laterite towers; statues, lingas"). IK 480.04; CISARK 1150; helloangkor Ref HA12261.
- Photos (2 only — Samnang may add more later): cover = close frontal view of the tower in full sun (polished, slight warm-cast correction). Second photo = wider shaded view showing the tower among trees with a neighboring tower visible behind (polished, no colour correction needed).
- Still to confirm: precise date (12th-13th c. vs. 11th-12th c., per conflict above), whether this is definitely the JASA-restored N1 tower or a different north tower, any linga/yoni finds specific to N1, exact 1908/1950s/1994-2005 restoration scope for N1 vs. the complex as a whole.

### 17. Banteay Thom Temple (ប្រាសាទបន្ទាយធំ)
- About 15 km north of Siem Reap and 10 km north of Angkor Wat, off the standard Small and Grand Circuits. In Kôk Kreul village, Leang Dai commune, Angkor Thom district, Siem Reap province (helloangkor). Reached by dirt trail (motorbike/bicycle/walking only); potholes and mud after rain.
- Khmer name used by Samnang: ប្រាសាទបន្ទាយធំ — matches helloangkor's Khmer site name (បា្រសាទបន្ទាយធំ, a transposition typo on their site for ប្រាសាទ). No spelling conflict.
- Date: 12th-13th century, believed built under King Jayavarman VII (helloangkor states this as the general belief, not confirmed by inscription on the page). No separate Wikipedia article found for this temple specifically; general Jayavarman VII-era context (hospitals, Bayon-style building program) corroborates the period but doesn't confirm the builder for this specific site.
- Architecture: faces east; cruciform terrace leads to the single gap in the outer laterite wall; two ponds beyond the gap; inner galleried wall encloses the main temple, 2 small libraries, and 3 ornately carved towers (now largely ruined). Carvings — pediments, devatas, dvarapalas, roundels — compared by helloangkor's author to Preah Khan's carving style. One near-intact pediment appears to show the Buddha's Great Departure (helloangkor interpretation, not stated as certain). Trees overgrow parts of the wall, compared to Ta Prohm.
- Moat: three-quarter moat around the inner galleried wall (not a full moat).
- Inscriptions (7 recorded): K.458, K.459, K.550, K.551, K.962, K.1038, K.1039. K.550/K.551 on door jambs of the north library and south tower, naming people of the royal order. K.962 on the east gopura 1 door jamb. K.1038 on the rear of the central tower's lintel. K.1039 on the central tower's door jamb. Several dated to the 12th century (no further precision given on the page).
- Other names: Prasat Trapeang Rondeas Thom, Rondas Thom, Neak Ta Bantay Thom, Pr. Banteai Thom ou Pr. Trapeang Rondeas Thom.
- Records: E. Lunet de Lajonquière 1911 (Inventaire III, p.135, IK 520, listed there as "Prasat Trapeang Rondeas Thom"). 1926 Liste des Monuments Historiques #409: "Temple with 3 sanctuaries and sandstone galleries bearing inscriptions." No restoration history found on helloangkor.
- Photos: user sent 4 photos, all from essentially the same standing position (same two towers, same angle, only exposure/zoom varying) — per project rule on near-identical photos, reduced to 3 distinct images rather than publishing near-duplicates: (1) cover = widest, best-lit full view of the twin towers plus a third tower visible at right; (2) cropped from a second original to isolate a carved pillar/devata fragment visible at the frame's left edge (unique content not in the others); (3) cropped tight on the left tower's doorway and carving detail as a zoomed close-up. One of the 4 originals was dropped entirely as a true near-duplicate with no unique content.
- Still to confirm: exact builder (Jayavarman VII is "believed," not inscription-confirmed on the page), precise inscription dates beyond "12th century" for several, restoration history (none found), identification of the Great Departure pediment (helloangkor's own interpretation).
