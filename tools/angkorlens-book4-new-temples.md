## Notes

### 1. Prasat Chrung Southeast (ប្រាសាទជ្រុង-អាគ្នេយ៍)
- Location: owner's Google Maps pin 13.4279237, 103.8732482 (13°25'40.5"N 103°52'23.7"E). SE corner of the Angkor Thom wall, Sangkat Nokor Thum.
- Records (helloangkor HA12857): Khmer name on helloangkor បា្រសាទជុ្រង(ទិសអាគ្នេយ៍); MH 364 (1926), IK 472.01, CISARK 1718. Remains: outer enclosure, entry gopura, stele shelter, sanctuary. 20-min walk on the wall from the South Gate; sunrise spot.
- Stele K.597: Sanskrit, all 4 faces (A–D), Saka 12th c., poor condition (Coedès, IC IV p. 231).
- APSARA: all 4 Prasat Chrung built end of Jayavarman VII's reign, Bayon style, cruciform sanctuary + square stone pavilion; carvings of small celestial beings; pediments to Lokesvara and Buddhas; SE one best preserved; 1,500 m walk from the gate.
- Not on page: the conversion to Hindu worship under Jayavarman VIII (it is on the NE page from earlier sources; APSARA's page does not mention it — not repeated here). Content of K.597 not summarised (no source read).

### 2. Gate of the Dead (ខ្លោងទ្វារខ្មោច)
- Location: owner's Google Maps pin 13.4414661, 103.8732166 (13°26'29.3"N 103°52'23.6"E), added 24 Nov 2026. Cover changed to sf-gatedead-3b (gate behind leaves) at owner's request.
- helloangkor HA12151: Thvear Khnoch / Khmoch; IK 473.02, CISARK 1009, MH 363; asuras/devas causeway remains (some likely in moat silt); two laterite chambers either side (purpose unknown); medallion band; original sandstone paving with wheel ruts; not on modern road network; statues found nearby by Henri Marchal 1925 (Hevajra, Prajnaparamita head, Buddha head, two Avalokitesvara); wall walk 7 min to Victory Gate, 18 min to SE Prasat Chrung. Refs: BEFEO Chronique 25 & 33; Rapport de la Conservation d'Angkor, May 1925.
- Wikipedia (Angkor Thom): east gate = Gate of the Dead; Victory Gate 500 m north; 23 m face towers; Bayon style, Jayavarman VII.
- Name origin: sources disagree (funeral processions / defeated armies / simply the east entrance) — page says it is uncertain.
- Lonely Planet: Tomb Raider filming location.
- Steel cables around the face tower: from the owner's photos only (no source found on who/when) — page only says they hold the blocks.

### 4. Spean Thma (ស្ពានថ្ម)
- Location: owner's Google Maps pin 13.446191, 103.8795661 (13°26'46.3"N 103°52'46.4"E). ~200 m east of Thommanon (Lonely Planet, Tourism Cambodia, Angkor Guide).
- Wikipedia (Rooney 2005 p.313): former course of Siem Reap River between Angkor Thom and East Baray; probably rebuilt after the Khmer period (~15th c.), reused sandstone blocks; 14 arches 1.10 m wide.
- Angkor Guide (Glaize): piers 1.60 m; cleared by H. Marchal 1920; bridge about twice as wide as the river.
- helloangkor HA11676: IK 491, MH 381 (1926 "ruined bridge"), CISARK 392; blocks with Bayon-era carving; helloangkor gives "17th century".
- Penang travel tips: "16th century". Phnom Penh Post: "originally constructed in Angkorian times". → Date conflict; page says sources disagree.
- National Geographic (Dan Penny): eastern end destroyed; floods; river went around the bridge.
- Ta Keo nursery waterwheel nearby (Phnom Penh Post, helloangkor).
- Lonely Planet says "an arch and several piers remain" vs. 14 arches elsewhere — page says "the remains show 14 arches" (Glaize/Wikipedia).

### 5. Victory Gate (ក្លោងទ្វារជ័យ)
- Location: owner's Google Maps pin 13.4460678, 103.8729782 (13°26'45.8"N 103°52'22.7"E).
- helloangkor HA11654: IK 473.01, CISARK 370, MH 363; inscriptions K.889, K.890, K.896, K.1018 (12th c. graffiti); axis to Terrace of Elephants / Royal Palace, East Baray terrace ~1.5 km, East Mebon ~5 km; 54 giants naga railing; recycled sandstone causeway; vajra figure (Marchal: Indra + 2 divinities; others: Jayavarman VII + 2 queens); only gate with pediment remains (Avalokiteshvara, west side); 1920 devas reassembled (BEFEO).
- Wikipedia: 500 m north of the east gate; 23 m towers. Smarthistory: used by the king to leave/return to the palace ("would have been" — page says "it is thought").
- NOT on page: helloangkor says "three elephants flank each side"; page uses the standard "three-headed elephants" (Glaize/Angkor Guide).

### 6. Ta Kav Gate (ខ្លោងទ្វារតាកៅ)
- Location: owner's Google Maps pin 13.4410643, 103.8447422 (13°26'27.8"N 103°50'41.1"E).
- helloangkor HA12153: IK 473.04, CISARK 1011, MH 363 (as "Angkor Thom"); laterite spurs; 54 asuras/devas each side; reused sandstone in causeway; giants' heads/torsos found in restoration; mixed basement mouldings (early 11th–12th c. bands); restoration led by all-female team (Thmey Thmey) with Heng Development support; wooden beams and sacred deposit stone with gold sheet found; newly sealed road; sunset spot.
- APSARA 8 Feb 2023: restoration 100% complete; post holes in laterite/sandstone floor (wooden structures). APSARA Oct 2022: 97%, new sandstone replacing lost stone, private support (Heng Development / Seang Chanheng). Phnom Penh Post: damage from age, climate, war, trees and roots.
- START DATE CONFLICT: APSARA says "since December 2019" in one article and "since the end of February 2021" in another → page gives only the completion date (Feb 2023).
- "All-female team" NOT on page (single secondary source).
- Ta Kav vs Ta Keo name: sources don't explain → not on page.

### 8. Prasat Chamreh (ប្រាសាទចំរេះ)
- Location: owner's Google Maps pin 13.7750555, 104.5545003 (13°46'30.2"N 104°33'16.2"E), Koh Ker.
- helloangkor HA11967 (as Op On / Chamreh): IK 274.02, CISARK 684, MH 678; brick on laterite base, east door with sandstone frame, false doors with pilasters/brick arches; false storeys; pierced stones for velum cord; chimney vault; lintel Indra on Airavata with garlands, frieze in niches (Parmentier 1939, type III); 300 m E of Banteay Pir Chan, ~500 m SE of Rahal.
- kingjayavarmanvii blog: lintel damaged, two blocks; scholars suggest a few years after 941. IntoCambodia: reign of Jayavarman IV; also called "Leak Neang" → DATE CONFLICT noted on page; Leak Neang alias NOT used (confusing with Koh Ker's Prasat Leak Neang).
- IntoCambodia says "100 m east of Prasat Lom Neang" vs Parmentier 300 m E of Banteay Pir Chan → only Parmentier used.
- Owner's photos show side/back with false doors; lintel not photographed.

### 9. Prasat Kravan (ប្រាសាទក្រវ៉ាន់)
- Location: owner's Google Maps pin 13.41974, 103.8998229 (13°25'11.1"N 103°53'59.4"E). Matches Wikipedia (13.41972, 103.89972).
- APSARA: Vishnu statue dedicated 12 Dec 921 by a court dignitary; moat, small causeway on west; vegetal binder; sandstone only for door frames, lintels, octagonal colonnettes; dvarapalas in niches; EFEO restoration early–mid 1960s.
- Wikipedia: 921 doorjamb inscription; founders high officials under Harshavarman I OR Ishanavarman II (page says sources disagree); Vishnu on Garuda / Vamana step / eight-armed; Lakshmi in north tower; brick reliefs common in Cham, rare in Khmer; 1930s clearing (Marchal, Trouvé); 1962–66 Groslier, bricks marked "CA".
- Bluffton (Sullivan): south-portal lintel Vishnu on Garuda with praying figures.
- RESTORATION DATE CONFLICT: Lonely Planet "partially restored in 1968" vs Wikipedia 1962–66 / APSARA early-to-mid 60s → page says "in the 1960s".
- Name meaning: Wikipedia "artabotrys odoratissimus" vs common "cardamom" → page EN says "cardamom temple"; KM text omits meaning.
- "CA" brick visible in owner's Lakshmi-wall photo.

### 10. Prasat Reamker / Dang Tong (ប្រាសាទរាមកេរ្តិ៍ / ដងទង់)

- On-site NAPV panel (2025, owner's photo): "Prasat Dang Tong (PKK23)"; brick + laterite, faces east, before 921 CE, Jayavarman IV, Shiva; local people: Ardhanarishvara statue; built before move from Angkor to Koh Ker; reconstruction drawing with vaulted roof.
- helloangkor HA11979 "Prasat Dang Tong (north)", "Prasat Reamker in some records", also Kok Khmaoch: elongated E–W brick prasat, corbelled roof (Parmentier 1939), north face bare without false door, lintel good condition (type III), octagonal colonnettes, mitred door frame, ornate pedestal, lion before steps; IK 281.10, CISARK 696, MH 690.

- Lintel description (kala, small seated figure, garland) from owner's photos; central figure not identified.

### 11. West Prasat Top (ប្រាសាទទាបលិច)
- Location: owner's Google Maps pin 13.4392, 103.85179 (13°26'21.1"N 103°51'06.4"E).
- Nara Nabunken leaflet: central + northern + southern sanctuaries, Buddhist terrace east, laterite enclosure + sema stones; one of last Angkor-period monuments; 14th-c. ritual goods, late-14th-c. ceramics; inscription related to Yasovarman I (late 9th c.); survey from 2002; ~40 stones fell May 2008; southern sanctuary 2012–Sep 2015; south stairs of central sanctuary found under southern sanctuary platform.
- Nabunken report: name means "small temple in the west", not original; ~50 m south of road to West Gate; earth-touching Buddhas on pediments, standing Buddhas on north-sanctuary false doors.
- APSARA 2024: Nara + APSARA; pink sandstone lintels/pillars; 10th c. origin, annexed 13th–17th c.
- Canby: 9th–17th c., post-Bayon, Theravada. actionstour: late 13th c.
- DISTANCE CONFLICT: Nabunken 500 m SW / Angkor Database 250 m SW / APSARA 700 m → page says only "southwest of the Bayon".
- DATE CONFLICT: page gives range and says sources differ.
- UCL paper says "Mangalartha or West Prasat Top ... 1295" — Mangalartha is EAST Prasat Top (Nabunken) → NOT used.
- Khmer name ទាបលិច from Angkor Database ("Prasat Tep Lech, the short temple to the west").
