Spean Khvav (Ancient Bridge)

Spean Khvav (ស្ពានខ្វាវ), also recorded as Spean Khvao, is an ancient laterite bridge on the east side of Khvav village, in Khvav commune, Chi Kraeng district, Siem Reap province. It stands on the old East Royal Road that linked Angkor with Beng Mealea and Preah Khan of Kompong Svay, a route that a modern road still follows today. The bridge is recorded as about 26 metres long, 7 metres wide and 3.2 metres high, built of laterite blocks with seven narrow corbelled arches, and is listed as dating from the twelfth century.

Each pier is made of stacked laterite blocks that step outward toward the top to close the arch, the typical Khmer way of building bridges without true arches. Fragments of the original sandstone naga balustrade still lie along the top, and one carved headpiece survives at the end of the bridge. It shows a seated figure sheltered by the many raised heads of a naga, and local people honour it today with a red cloth. Hello Angkor describes the bridge as in amazing condition for its age.

Spean Khvav is one of several ancient bridges around Khvav commune on the same royal road. It was included in the 1926 list of Historic Monuments (no. 222, "Spean Khvao", a bridge in the Kompong Svay region) and is registered as IK 233.01 and CISARK 1599.
