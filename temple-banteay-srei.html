Banteay Srei

ប្រាសាទបន្ទាយស្រី

Cambodia

Photographs by Samnang Loy · AngkorLens

Founded: 967 CE (foundation inscription) · Dedicated to: Shiva · Built by: Yajnavaraha, a Brahmin advisor, and his brother Vishnukumara · Stone: Pink sandstone

Banteay Srei is a small temple north of the main Angkor temples, famous for the finest stone carving in Khmer art. Its name is often translated as “citadel of women” or “citadel of beauty.”

According to its foundation inscription, it was founded in 967 CE during the reign of King Rajendravarman II. Unlike most great temples, it was not built by a king but by his Brahmin advisor Yajnavaraha and his brother Vishnukumara, and was dedicated to Shiva.

The temple is built of pink sandstone, which turns warm orange and red in the afternoon sun. Three small towers stand in the centre, and the walls and gateways are covered in deep, lace-like carving.

Look up at the triangular pediments: rows of flame-like leaves, lotus flowers in diamond frames, and at the ends, great curling tails shaped like waves.

The temple was rediscovered in 1914. In 1923 the French writer André Malraux was caught trying to take carved devatas away. From 1931, Henri Marchal restored it by anastylosis, putting the original stones back in place.

Inside, the carvings tell stories. One pediment shows a scene from the Ramayana: the monkey brothers Vali and Sugriva fighting, while Rama aims his bow. Another, from the Mahabharata, shows the god Indra on his three-headed elephant sending rain over the burning Khandava forest, full of fleeing animals. On another pediment, Narasimha, the lion-man form of Vishnu, tears apart the demon king Hiranyakashipu.

Kneeling guardian figures with animal heads sit beside the stairs of the central shrines, and graceful devatas smile from their niches. Even the false doors are carved stone, covered with tiny flowers and patterns.

Look for: The three central towers · the curling pediment ends against the sky · the lotus flowers in diamond frames · the deep pink colour of the stone · the tall white tree behind the temple.
