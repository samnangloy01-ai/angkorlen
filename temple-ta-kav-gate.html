Ta Kav Gate (West Gate of Angkor Thom)

Ta Kav Gate (ខ្លោងទ្វារតាកៅ), also recorded as Takav, TaKaov or Thvear Ta Keo, is the west gate of Angkor Thom, one of the five gates through the walls of the city founded by King Jayavarman VII in the late twelfth century. It lies directly west of the Bayon. Like the other gates, it is crowned by face towers in the style of the Bayon, with smiling faces looking out in the four directions, and three-headed elephants stand at the corners of the doorway. Large laterite spurs project from the wall on both sides of the entrance.

In front of the gate, a causeway crosses the moat with a naga railing held by rows of gods (devas) and demons (asuras), the scene of the Churning of the Ocean of Milk. Part of the causeway was built from sandstone reused from an earlier temple, and several heads and bodies of the stone giants were found during restoration. The gate's base mouldings are unusual: they mix several decorative bands in styles from the early eleventh to the twelfth century, while the other gates use one uniform band. This suggests an earlier structure or reused stone.

For a long time the west gate was one of the most ruined of the five, damaged by age, climate, war, and trees and roots growing from the stones. The APSARA National Authority restored it with private support from Heng Development, replacing lost or broken stones with new sandstone; the lighter new blocks can still be seen in the faces today. APSARA announced the restoration complete in February 2023. During the work, excavations at the entrance found post holes in the laterite and sandstone floor, probably for wooden structures, and a sacred deposit stone with a gold sheet. A sealed road now leads to the gate, and it is a popular place at sunset, when the low sun turns the west-facing stones gold. It is listed as IK 473.04 and CISARK 1011.
