Prasat Preah Phnom

ប្រាសាទព្រះភ្នំ

Five brick towers · Angkor Chum, Siem Reap

Photographs by Samnang Loy · AngkorLens

Prasat Preah Phnom — ប្រាសាទព្រះភ្នំ ("The Holy Mountain")

Location: Preah Phnom village, Srae Khvav commune, Angkor Chum district, Siem Reap province — about 65 km from Siem Reap town.

Preah Phnom is a 10th-century temple of five square brick sanctuaries standing on a natural mound and facing east. The towers are set in two north–south rows: three larger towers in front and two smaller ones behind. Two brick annexes ("libraries") stand nearby, now mostly ruined. A moat once surrounded the site — today dry and partly farmed — and a large rectangular basin lies to the north. No enclosure wall or gateway survives.

The front towers keep their sandstone door frames with octagonal colonnettes and their carved lintels, though the carving is heavily worn. On the lintel of the central tower the god Indra riding his three-headed elephant can still be recognized. The corbelled brick roofs, with their stepped false storeys, are still standing, crowned today with grass and small trees. The temple has never been restored — what you see is the original structure as it has survived.

Three inscriptions have been found here. K. 593 is dated Saka 852 (930 CE) and K. 454, on the central tower, Saka 1023 (1101 CE); a third, K. 594, has 15 lines. The temple was described by Lunet de Lajonquière in 1901 (Inventaire Khmer no. 706) and listed as a historic monument in 1926 (no. 565).
